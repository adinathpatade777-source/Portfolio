# Doodle Dollar — "POV: You Skipped the Car Loan"

**Format:** Long-form doodle/stickman explainer · ~6–7 min · ~950 words of voiceover
**Title options:**
1. POV: You Skipped the Car Loan (and Your Friend Didn't)
2. This Truck Cost Him $754,000
3. Two Friends, One Car Decision, 36 Years Later

**Thumbnail:** Left: stickman in a shiny red truck, sweating, chained to a giant "$750/mo" sign. Right: stickman in a dented old hatchback, smiling, sitting on a pile of money. Big text: "$754,000 MISTAKE?"

**Characters:**
- **Sam** — red cap, loves shiny things.
- **Alex** — blue hoodie, calm, slightly boring (on purpose).
- **The Payment** — a small green blob monster with "$750" on its belly. It grows every time it eats.

---

## 0:00 — HOOK

[VISUAL: White page. A pencil draws two identical stickmen standing side by side, then labels them SAM and ALEX.]

**VO:** Two friends. Same age. Same job. Same paycheck.

[VISUAL: A car dealership sign pops in. Sam's eyes turn into hearts.]

**VO:** And one day, they both need a car. This one decision is going to be worth seven hundred and fifty-four thousand dollars. And only one of them knows it.

[VISUAL: "$754,000" stamps onto the screen, then cracks.]

**VO:** You're Alex. Let's see what happens.

---

## 0:25 — THE CHOICE

[VISUAL: Sam at the dealership, next to a huge glowing red truck. A salesman stickman with a giant grin.]

**VO:** Sam walks in "just to look." Walks out with a forty-five thousand dollar truck.

**SALESMAN (speech bubble):** Don't worry about the price. Just the monthly!

[VISUAL: A contract unrolls like a scroll: "72 MONTHS · $750/month".]

**VO:** Seven hundred fifty a month. For six years. Sam feels amazing.

[VISUAL: Cut to Alex in a used-car lot, kicking the tire of a small, dented hatchback. Price tag: "$12,000 · CASH".]

**VO:** You — Alex — buy a used hatchback. Twelve thousand dollars, paid with savings. It has a weird smell and one door that squeaks.

[VISUAL: Door squeaks. Alex shrugs.]

**VO:** But here's the one thing you do differently. Every month, you pretend you have a car payment too.

[VISUAL: Alex drops $750 into a glass jar labeled "INVESTED — index fund".]

---

## 1:20 — YEAR 1: SAM IS WINNING

[VISUAL: Split screen. Sam's side: confetti, friends taking selfies with the truck, a "LIKE" counter climbing. Alex's side: grey, quiet, Alex eating noodles in the hatchback.]

**VO:** Year one, Sam is clearly winning. Everyone loves the truck. Nobody posts photos of a hatchback.

[VISUAL: The Payment monster appears for the first time, tiny and cute, sitting on Sam's shoulder.]

**VO:** And Sam meets a new friend. The Payment. It eats seven hundred fifty dollars every month. It seems harmless.

[VISUAL: Alex's jar shows "$9,300".]

**VO:** Your jar? About nine thousand dollars. Nobody claps.

---

## 2:00 — THE RAISE

[VISUAL: Both stickmen get an envelope: "RAISE! +$300/month".]

**VO:** Year two. Both of you get a raise.

[VISUAL: Sam opens the envelope — The Payment snatches it and swallows it, growing bigger. Labels fly off: "insurance ↑", "gas", "new tires".]

**VO:** But a big truck has big friends: higher insurance, more gas, pricier tires. Sam's raise disappears before it ever feels like a raise.

[VISUAL: Alex's raise goes half into the jar, half into a small "fun" box with a pizza drawn on it.]

**VO:** Your raise? Half goes to the jar. Half goes to pizza. You're allowed to enjoy life — that's not the point.

---

## 2:40 — THE MAGIC TRICK (COMPOUNDING)

[VISUAL: Zoom into Alex's jar. Coins inside start sprouting tiny baby coins.]

**VO:** Here's what's happening inside your jar. Your money earns a return. Then that return earns a return. Then *that* return earns a return.

[VISUAL: A snowball rolling down a hill, getting bigger, with dollar signs stuck to it.]

**VO:** It's called compounding. At first it's boring. Painfully boring. Then it's not.

[VISUAL: On-screen text: "We'll assume 8% average yearly return. Real markets go up AND down."]

**VO:** For this story, we'll assume an eight percent average yearly return — roughly what a broad stock index has done over long periods. Not guaranteed. Some years it drops. But over decades, this is the bet.

---

## 3:30 — YEAR 6: THE FINISH LINE?

[VISUAL: Calendar flipping fast. Sam's truck now has scratches, a dent, a coffee stain on the seat. A "VALUE" tag on it drops: $45,000 → $18,000.]

**VO:** Six years later. Sam makes the last payment. Seventy-two payments. Fifty-four thousand dollars, including interest.

[VISUAL: The truck's price tag shrinks to "$18,000".]

**VO:** And the truck? Now worth about eighteen thousand.

[VISUAL: Alex's jar is now big, overflowing: "$69,000".]

**VO:** Your jar? Sixty-nine thousand dollars. Same money went out of both your pockets every month. One of you has a used truck. The other has sixty-nine thousand dollars *and* a used car.

---

## 4:15 — THE TRAP

[VISUAL: Sam at the dealership again. The salesman's grin is even bigger. A new, even shinier truck.]

**VO:** Now here's where most people fall into the trap.

**SALESMAN:** Trade it in! Your payment barely changes!

[VISUAL: The Payment monster, now huge, high-fives the salesman.]

**VO:** Sam trades in the old truck and starts a brand-new loan. The Payment never leaves. It just gets a new name every six years.

[VISUAL: A loop arrow: "New truck → 6 years of payments → trade in → new truck…"]

---

## 4:45 — THE BORING MOVE THAT WINS

[VISUAL: Alex standing next to the jar, holding a "STOP" sign over a calendar.]

**VO:** And you? You do the most boring thing possible. You stop adding to the jar — and you just… leave it alone.

[VISUAL: Timelapse: Alex gets older, grey hair, glasses. The jar becomes a barrel, then a building-sized vault.]

**VO:** You don't add another dollar. For thirty years. You just let the snowball roll.

[VISUAL: Number counter spinning up: $69,000 → $150,000 → $340,000 → $754,000.]

**VO:** Thirty years later, that sixty-nine thousand has grown to about seven hundred fifty-four thousand dollars.

[VISUAL: Old Sam next to old Alex. Sam is in yet another new truck, The Payment monster is now the size of a house behind him.]

**VO:** Sam's had five or six shiny trucks. You've had a squeaky door and three-quarters of a million dollars.

---

## 5:30 — THE TWIST: IT'S NOT ABOUT CARS

[VISUAL: The trucks and hatchback fade away. Only the two stickmen remain on the white page.]

**VO:** Now — this isn't about trucks. Sam isn't a bad guy. And if you need a reliable car for work or family, buy a reliable car.

[VISUAL: Three icons appear: a phone with "$1,200", a sofa with "0% financing!", a vacation with "Buy now, pay later".]

**VO:** It's about The Payment. Any monthly payment for something that loses value — a phone, a sofa, buy-now-pay-later shoes — is a little monster that eats your future raises.

[VISUAL: Alex drops one coin in a small jar. The coin glows.]

**VO:** Every dollar you don't hand to The Payment can go to work for you instead.

---

## 6:05 — CALL TO ACTION

[VISUAL: Text writes itself on screen, one line at a time.]

**VO:** So here's your challenge for this week:
1. Write down every monthly payment you have.
2. Circle the ones that pay for things that lose value.
3. Pick one. Imagine that money going into a jar for thirty years instead.

[VISUAL: The Payment monster shrinks and runs off the page, crying.]

**VO:** You don't have to be boring forever. Just boring *enough*.

[VISUAL: Doodle Dollar logo — a dollar bill with a pencil — draws itself.]

**VO:** I'm Doodle Dollar. If this made money feel a little simpler, subscribe — and tell me in the comments: what's the monthly payment you're going to kill first?

**[END — ~6:30]**

## Description (for YouTube)

Two friends, same salary, one car decision. One takes a $45,000 truck loan at $750/month. The other buys a $12,000 used car with cash and invests the same $750 every month. Thirty-six years later, the difference is about $754,000.

In this doodle explainer: how car payments eat your raises, how compounding works, and the "monthly payment trap" that keeps people broke.

## Number check (for the editor)

- Loan: $750/month × 72 months = **$54,000** paid in total.
- Alex invests $750/month for 72 months at 8%/yr (monthly compounding) ≈ **$69,000**.
- That $69,000 left untouched for 30 more years at 8% ≈ **$754,800**.
- Truck resale after 6 years (~$18,000) is an illustrative estimate.
